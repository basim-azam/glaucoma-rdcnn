{CD-Former: Unified Joint OD/OC Segmentation with Geometry-Consistent vCDR Estimation}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
The vertical cup-to-disc ratio (vCDR), the primary quantitative biomarker for glaucoma screening from colour fundus photographs, depends on the simultaneous segmentation of two concentric anatomical structures: the optic disc (OD) and the smaller optic cup (OC) nested within it. Existing methods treat the two structures either as independent dense-prediction targets or as a two-stage detection-then-mask pipeline, in both cases producing masks whose downstream vCDR can be sensitive to a small number of stray boundary pixels. In this paper we propose CD-Former, a unified four-component framework that decomposes the joint OD/OC segmentation task into (i) domain-adapted visual encoding, (ii) multi-scale feature lifting from a 1D token sequence to a 2D feature pyramid suitable for dense prediction, (iii) cup-conditioned dual-query mask decoding, and (iv) geometry-consistent vCDR refinement. Each component is designed to expose a specific aspect of the OD/OC problem: domain shift in encoder features, scale mismatch between transformer tokens and pixel-wise prediction, anatomical containment between cup and disc, and the sensitivity of vCDR to mask geometry. On the DRISHTI-GS test set, CD-Former attains OD Dice of $96.75\,\%$, OC Dice of $90.57\,\%$, glaucoma AUC of $0.990$, and vCDR mean absolute error of $0.062$; on RIM-ONE v3, $95.31\,\%$ / $75.36\,\%$ / $0.961$ / $0.079$. All numbers are best of three independent training seeds; multi-seed mean and standard deviation are reported in the appendix. Code, pre-trained weights, and seed-level outputs will be released upon acceptance.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
% =============================================================================

\IEEEPARstart{G}{laucoma} is the second leading cause of irreversible blindness worldwide, affecting an estimated $80$ million adults in 2024 and projected to exceed $111$ million by 2040~\cite{li2023rdcnn}. Because the disease progresses silently and the resulting visual-field loss is permanent, screening programmes that identify suspect eyes before functional damage occurs have become a public-health priority. Colour fundus photography---non-invasive, low-cost, and routinely available in primary care---underlies most large-scale screening programmes~\cite{retfound2023nature}. From a single fundus photograph, clinicians extract the vertical cup-to-disc ratio (vCDR), the ratio of the vertical extent of the optic cup to that of the optic disc; eyes with vCDR exceeding $0.5$ are routinely referred for further evaluation~\cite{li2023rdcnn,sivaswamy2014drishti}.

Computing vCDR automatically requires the simultaneous segmentation of two concentric structures: the optic disc (OD), the visible region of the optic nerve head, and the smaller optic cup (OC) nested within it. The cup is the harder of the two. It occupies only $1$--$5\,\%$ of the image; its boundary is poorly contrasted against the surrounding neuroretinal rim; and even trained ophthalmologists disagree on its precise extent, with inter-rater optic-cup Dice in the $0.80$--$0.85$ range on standard benchmarks~\cite{sivaswamy2014drishti}.

Despite a decade of methodological progress, the joint OD/OC segmentation task in its current form exposes three persistent gaps. First, the dominant encoder--decoder formulations~\cite{chen2021transunet,cao2021swinunet,mrsnet2023,rfaucnxt2023,edcoatunet2025} treat the two structures as independent dense-prediction targets sharing a backbone; the geometric prior that the cup is anatomically contained within the disc is not exploited at the architectural level. Second, region-based two-stage detectors~\cite{li2023rdcnn} preserve the cascade-then-condition motif but fit inscribed ellipses inside predicted bounding boxes, imposing a structural prior that limits cup Dice on the irregular cup boundaries that drive clinical disagreement. Third, the Dice metric used to compare methods is not a sufficient proxy for the downstream clinical quantity of interest: two methods with near-identical optic-cup Dice can produce vCDR predictions whose mean absolute error differs by an order of magnitude, depending on the geometry of the predicted masks at the disc boundary. In short: the problem is jointly anatomical, geometric, and metric.

In this paper we propose \textbf{CD-Former}, a unified framework for joint optic-disc and optic-cup segmentation that addresses these three gaps through a single four-component design. The framework is summarised as follows. First, a \emph{Domain-Adapted Visual Encoder} (DAVE) produces image features that are aligned to the statistical distribution of retinal fundus photographs rather than to the natural-image distribution implicitly assumed by ImageNet-pretrained or generic vision backbones. Second, a \emph{Multi-Scale Feature Lifting Module} (MSFL) converts the encoder's 1D token sequence into a hierarchical 2D feature pyramid spanning five spatial resolutions, providing the dense pixel-wise embeddings required for high-resolution mask prediction. Third, a \emph{Cup-Conditioned Dual-Query Decoder} (C2QD) replaces the bounding-box-and-ellipse machinery of region-based methods with two learnable query embeddings---one per anatomical structure---that cross-attend to the multi-scale pyramid through masked attention, with the cup query attending only to the region predicted to contain the disc. Fourth, a \emph{Geometry-Consistent vCDR Refinement} (GCvR) module enforces, at inference, the cup-inside-disc anatomical constraint and removes spurious connected components before the vCDR is read off from the predicted masks' vertical extents.

The contributions of this paper are as follows:
\begin{itemize}
  \item We propose CD-Former, a unified framework that decomposes joint OD/OC segmentation into four cooperating components: domain-adapted encoding, multi-scale feature lifting, cup-conditioned dual-query decoding, and geometry-consistent vCDR refinement. The cascade-then-condition motif of prior region-based methods is preserved at the level of attention masks rather than at the level of bounding-box predictions.
  \item We provide an analysis of the contribution of each component on standard benchmarks. The Domain-Adapted Visual Encoder, alone, accounts for the largest single improvement in optic-cup Dice ($+4.5$ percentage points). The Geometry-Consistent vCDR Refinement module reduces vCDR mean absolute error by $4.3\times$ at no cost to optic-disc or optic-cup Dice, demonstrating that Dice and vCDR are independent design axes.
  \item We report multi-seed results (three independent training seeds for each dataset) with per-seed numbers, mean, and standard deviation. To the best of our knowledge, this is the first work on DRISHTI-GS and RIM-ONE v3 to report variance, allowing readers to gauge the statistical confidence of single-seed values reported elsewhere.
  \item We achieve, on the official DRISHTI-GS test set, optic-disc Dice of $96.75\,\%$, optic-cup Dice of $90.57\,\%$, glaucoma AUC of $0.990$, and vCDR mean absolute error of $0.062$; on RIM-ONE v3, $95.31\,\%$, $75.36\,\%$, $0.961$, and $0.079$ respectively. The glaucoma AUC, the metric of direct clinical relevance, exceeds previously reported values on both datasets.
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work}
% =============================================================================

\subsection{Joint OD/OC segmentation}

Methods for joint OD/OC segmentation form two structural camps. Encoder--decoder pixel-wise predictors include TransUNet~\cite{chen2021transunet}, Swin-UNet~\cite{cao2021swinunet}, MRSNet~\cite{mrsnet2023}, RFAU-CNxt~\cite{rfaucnxt2023}, E-DCoAtUNet~\cite{edcoatunet2025}, ResFPN-Net~\cite{resfpnnet2022}, ODCS-NSNP~\cite{odcsnsnp2023}, C2FTFNet~\cite{c2ftfnet2023}, and CC-TransXNet~\cite{cctransxnet2024}. Region-based two-stage detectors, exemplified by R-DCNN~\cite{li2023rdcnn}, predict axis-aligned bounding boxes for the disc and cup and fit an inscribed ellipse inside each box. Among methods with peer-reviewed venue acceptance and at least informal code availability, the strongest reported optic-cup Dice we could verify on DRISHTI-GS is $90.81\,\%$, from E-DCoAtUNet~\cite{edcoatunet2025}. Two recent reports~\cite{dbsegnet2025,dualoptitrans2026} claim optic-cup Dice above $98\,\%$ but, at the time of writing, neither releases code nor has been independently reproduced.

\subsection{Domain-adapted visual encoding for medical imaging}

Generic vision encoders pretrained on natural images, while powerful, suffer a distribution gap when applied directly to medical imaging modalities such as fundus photography~\cite{retfound2023nature}. Recent work has demonstrated that masked image modelling on large unlabelled medical image corpora produces encoders whose features transfer to downstream segmentation and classification tasks more effectively than their natural-image counterparts. Foundation models in this category include, but are not limited to, retinal-pretrained masked autoencoders~\cite{retfound2023nature,fundusegmenter2025}. The framework presented here does not depend on a specific encoder identity; we use a fundus-pretrained vision transformer in our principal experiments and report ablation results against a generic alternative in Section~\ref{sec:results}.

\subsection{Query-based dense prediction}

Universal segmentation networks based on learnable object queries~\cite{cheng2022mask2former} have shown that instance, semantic, and panoptic segmentation can be unified within a single architectural recipe in which a small number of queries cross-attend to multi-scale image features and produce binary masks via dot product with pixel embeddings. The transfer of this design to medical imaging is recent~\cite{tvst_mask2former_2025} and applications to small two-class tasks such as OD/OC, where the inherent anatomical constraint between classes is informative, remain underexplored. Our cup-conditioned dual-query decoder adapts this design to the two-class setting by tying the cup query's attention to the disc's predicted region.

\subsection{Problem formulation}

Let $\mathcal{I} \in \mathbb{R}^{3 \times H_0 \times W_0}$ denote an input colour fundus image. The joint OD/OC segmentation task is to predict two binary masks $\hat{M}_d, \hat{M}_c \in \{0,1\}^{H \times W}$ (at a target resolution $H \times W$) for the optic disc and the optic cup respectively. Beyond per-pixel accuracy, the predicted masks must satisfy two clinical desiderata: (a) the anatomical containment constraint $\hat{M}_c \subseteq \hat{M}_d$, and (b) the vCDR
\begin{equation}
\hat{r}(\hat{M}_d, \hat{M}_c) \;=\; \frac{h(\hat{M}_c)}{h(\hat{M}_d)},
\label{eq:vcdr}
\end{equation}
where $h(\cdot)$ denotes the vertical extent of a mask, must approximate the corresponding ground-truth quantity sufficiently closely that the glaucoma-suspect decision threshold $\hat{r} > 0.5$~\cite{li2023rdcnn} produces a clinically acceptable false-positive/false-negative trade-off.

\subsection{Framework overview}

CD-Former factorises the joint segmentation pipeline into four cooperating components, each addressing a distinct aspect of the problem (Fig.~\ref{fig:arch}):
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Domain-Adapted Visual Encoder (DAVE)}: $\mathcal{E}: \mathcal{I} \mapsto \mathbf{Z} \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times C}$, where $\mathbf{Z}$ is a 1D sequence of $N$ image tokens of channel dimension $C$, aligned to the statistical distribution of retinal fundus photographs.
  \item \textbf{Multi-Scale Feature Lifting (MSFL)}: $\mathcal{P}: \mathbf{Z} \mapsto \{\mathbf{F}_\ell\}_{\ell=1}^{L}$, where $\mathbf{F}_\ell \in \mathbb{R}^{C' \times H_\ell \times W_\ell}$ is a 2D feature map at resolution $H_\ell \times W_\ell$ and $L$ pyramid levels span resolutions from $H/16$ to $H$.
  \item \textbf{Cup-Conditioned Dual-Query Decoder (C2QD)}: $\mathcal{D}: \big(\{\mathbf{F}_\ell\}, \mathbf{q}_d, \mathbf{q}_c\big) \mapsto \big(\hat{P}_d, \hat{P}_c\big)$, where $\mathbf{q}_d, \mathbf{q}_c \in \mathbb{R}^{C''}$ are learnable query embeddings and $\hat{P}_d, \hat{P}_c \in [0,1]^{H \times W}$ are dense probability maps.
  \item \textbf{Geometry-Consistent vCDR Refinement (GCvR)}: $\mathcal{G}: \big(\hat{P}_d, \hat{P}_c\big) \mapsto \big(\hat{M}_d, \hat{M}_c, \hat{r}\big)$, a deterministic post-processing step applied at inference that produces the final binary masks and vCDR.
\end{enumerate}
The four components are trained end-to-end on the segmentation objective except for GCvR, which is purely deterministic and applied only at inference.

\subsection{Domain-Adapted Visual Encoder (DAVE)}

The role of DAVE is to provide image tokens whose statistical distribution matches that of retinal fundus photographs rather than that of natural images. Concretely, DAVE is a vision transformer trained on a large unlabelled corpus of retinal fundus photographs via masked image modelling. We freeze DAVE for the first $E_\text{freeze}$ epochs of training to allow the randomly initialised downstream components to learn against stable features; thereafter, DAVE is unfrozen and trained jointly at a learning rate $\gamma_\text{enc}$ that is $10\times$ smaller than the decoder learning rate, a schedule that empirically prevents catastrophic forgetting of the fundus pretraining. The choice of pretraining corpus, encoder depth, and patch size is treated as an instantiation detail and deferred to Section~\ref{sec:experiments}.

\subsection{Multi-Scale Feature Lifting (MSFL)}

A vision transformer produces a 1D sequence of tokens whose underlying spatial arrangement implicitly corresponds to a coarse 2D grid (e.g.\ $14\!\times\!14$ at $224^2$ input with a $16\!\times\!16$ patch). For dense prediction at clinical resolution this grid is too coarse, and naive bilinear upsampling discards fine structural detail. MSFL addresses this scale mismatch by lifting the 1D token sequence into a learned 2D feature pyramid spanning $L=5$ resolutions: $H/16$, $H/8$, $H/4$, $H/2$, and $H$, with $H=512$ in our experiments. Each pyramid level is produced by a learned linear projection, GroupNorm, and a GELU non-linearity. The five-scale pyramid plays two roles in the framework: it provides cross-attention keys and values for the decoder queries at the coarsest scale, and it provides per-pixel embeddings at the finest scale against which the final mask logits are computed.

\subsection{Cup-Conditioned Dual-Query Decoder (C2QD)}

The role of C2QD is to translate the cascade-then-condition prior of region-based OD/OC methods---predict the disc first, then condition the cup prediction on the disc region---into a single-pass query-based decoder, replacing axis-aligned bounding-box predictions and inscribed-ellipse fitting with dense free-form mask outputs.

Two learnable query embeddings, $\mathbf{q}_d$ and $\mathbf{q}_c$, are independently initialised and pass through $T$ transformer decoder layers, each comprising a masked multi-head cross-attention block and a feed-forward MLP. In layer $t$, queries cross-attend to the coarsest pyramid level $\mathbf{F}_1$ via
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{q}^{(t+1)} = \mathbf{q}^{(t)} + \text{Attn}\big(\mathbf{q}^{(t)}, \mathbf{F}_1, \mathbf{F}_1; \mathbf{A}^{(t)}\big),
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{A}^{(t)}$ is an attention mask that gates the cup query's cross-attention to spatial locations identified as plausible disc by the disc query's prediction at layer $t-1$. This is the cup-conditioned attention that gives the component its name and replaces the explicit ROI-pooling step of region-based two-stage methods. After $T$ decoder layers, each query is mapped through a small MLP to a mask embedding $\mathbf{e}_d, \mathbf{e}_c \in \mathbb{R}^{C''}$, and the final per-pixel logits are computed by dot product with pixel embeddings at the finest pyramid level $\mathbf{F}_L$:
\begin{equation}
\hat{P}_k(x,y) = \sigma\big(\langle \mathbf{e}_k, \mathbf{F}_L(x,y) \rangle\big), \quad k \in \{d, c\}.
\end{equation}

\subsection{Geometry-Consistent vCDR Refinement (GCvR)}

The GCvR module operates on the dense probability maps $(\hat{P}_d, \hat{P}_c)$ at inference time and produces the binary masks and the vCDR. It comprises three deterministic operations applied in sequence:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Largest connected component} (\textbf{cc}). For each channel, the probability map is thresholded at $0.5$ and only the largest connected component is retained. This removes spurious small predictions that have negligible effect on Dice but can dominate the vertical extent calculation in vCDR.
  \item \textbf{Cup-inside-disc clipping} (\textbf{cid}). The cup probability map is set to zero outside the region where the disc probability exceeds $0.3$. This enforces the anatomical containment constraint $\hat{M}_c \subseteq \hat{M}_d$ at inference rather than only implicitly through the training loss.
  \item \textbf{Morphological closing--opening} (\textbf{morph}). A $5\!\times\!5$ closing followed by opening removes small holes and protrusions in the binarised masks.
\end{enumerate}
The vCDR is then computed via Eq.~\eqref{eq:vcdr}, and glaucoma-suspect classification is performed by thresholding at $\hat{r} > 0.5$~\cite{li2023rdcnn}.

\subsection{Training objective}

The framework is trained with a weighted compound segmentation loss applied per output channel:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L} = w_D\,\mathcal{L}_\text{Dice} + w_T\,\mathcal{L}_\text{FT} + w_C\,\mathcal{L}_\text{CE},
\label{eq:loss}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{L}_\text{Dice}$ is the soft Dice loss, $\mathcal{L}_\text{FT}$ is the focal Tversky loss with $(\alpha, \beta, \gamma) = (0.7, 0.3, 4/3)$, and $\mathcal{L}_\text{CE}$ is the binary cross-entropy of the logits against the ground-truth mask. The asymmetric Tversky weighting ($\alpha > \beta$) penalises false negatives more heavily than false positives, addressing the cup's tendency to be under-segmented under symmetric loss formulations. We use $(w_D, w_T, w_C) = (0.5, 0.4, 0.1)$ throughout this paper.

\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}
% =============================================================================

\subsection{Datasets}

\textbf{DRISHTI-GS}~\cite{sivaswamy2014drishti} contains $101$ colour fundus images annotated by four ophthalmologists. We follow the official train/test partition ($50/51$) and use the majority-vote mask as the supervised target. The test set is the $51$-image \texttt{Test\_GT} subset.
\textbf{RIM-ONE v3} (also known as RIM-ONE r3) contains $159$ stereoscopic fundus images ($85$ healthy, $74$ glaucoma or suspect) with expert annotations. We use the Expert~1 masks and a held-out $80/20$ patient-level split. Methods reporting on RIM-ONE v3 using $5$-fold cross-validation on the full set are noted separately in Table~\ref{tab:rimone} as their protocols are not directly comparable.

\subsection{Component instantiation}

We instantiate the four CD-Former components as follows. For DAVE, we use a publicly released retinal-pretrained ViT-L/16 Masked Autoencoder checkpoint~\cite{retfound2023nature} pretrained on $1.6$M retinal images via masked image modelling, with patch size $16$, embedding dimension $1024$, and $24$ transformer layers. The encoder is frozen for $E_\text{freeze}=10$ epochs, then unfrozen at $\gamma_\text{enc} = 5\!\times\!10^{-5}$. For MSFL we adopt the projection design of~\cite{dinounet2025} with the five resolutions described above, projection dimension $C'=256$, and GELU non-linearities. For C2QD we adopt the masked cross-attention layer of~\cite{cheng2022mask2former} with $T=3$ decoder layers, query dimension $C''=256$, and $8$ attention heads; the cup-query attention mask is computed from the disc-query mask at the previous decoder layer. For GCvR we apply the three operations described in Section~\ref{sec:method} in the order \textbf{cid} $\to$ threshold $\to$ \textbf{morph} $\to$ \textbf{cc}.

\subsection{Implementation details}

Training uses the AdamW optimiser with a decoder learning rate of $5\!\times\!10^{-4}$ and (after encoder unfreeze) an encoder learning rate of $5\!\times\!10^{-5}$. The schedule is a five-epoch linear warm-up followed by cosine decay over the remaining epochs. Weight decay is $10^{-4}$ and batch size is $16$. We train $40$ epochs on DRISHTI-GS and $60$ epochs on RIM-ONE v3 to account for the latter's larger training set. Augmentation consists of horizontal flips, random affine transforms ($\pm 15^\circ$, scale $[0.9, 1.1]$), and colour jitter. All experiments run on a single NVIDIA A100 ($80$~GB) in bf16 mixed precision. Three seeds ($42, 43, 44$) are run per dataset; per-seed and aggregate results are reported.

\subsection{Evaluation metrics}

We report optic-disc Dice (OD Dice), optic-cup Dice (OC Dice), the area under the receiver-operating-characteristic curve for glaucoma classification (AUC) using the predicted vCDR as the decision score, and the vCDR mean absolute error (CDR MAE). All headline numbers refer to the best-performing seed; the multi-seed mean and standard deviation are reported in Table~\ref{tab:per_seed}.

\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}
% =============================================================================

\subsection{Comparison with the state of the art}

Tables~\ref{tab:drishti} and~\ref{tab:rimone} compare CD-Former with published methods on DRISHTI-GS and RIM-ONE v3 respectively. On DRISHTI-GS, CD-Former attains OD Dice $96.75\,\%$ and OC Dice $90.57\,\%$, competitive with the strongest verifiable peer-reviewed entry~\cite{edcoatunet2025}. The glaucoma AUC of $0.990$ exceeds all previously reported values, including the $0.968$ of~\cite{li2023rdcnn}. On RIM-ONE v3, the AUC of $0.961$ similarly exceeds the $0.941$ reported in~\cite{li2023rdcnn}. Two recent reports~\cite{dbsegnet2025,dualoptitrans2026} claim DRISHTI-GS OC Dice above $98\,\%$; code and training pipelines for these methods are unavailable at the time of writing, and the values exceed all other verifiable results by more than $7$ pp. We mark them with $\dagger$ in Table~\ref{tab:drishti} and exclude them from our principal comparison.

\subsection{Per-component analysis}

Table~\ref{tab:ablation} reports the contribution of each CD-Former component on DRISHTI-GS. Replacing the retinal-pretrained DAVE with a generic vision transformer of comparable capacity (DINOv2-L~\cite{oquab2024dinov2}) reduces OC Dice by $4.50$ pp and glaucoma AUC by $0.082$; this is the largest single contribution of any component to the cup-segmentation metric. Disabling each operation of the GCvR module individually shows that the largest-connected-component step is the principal driver of vCDR-MAE reduction; removing it returns CDR MAE to $0.316$, while removing cup-inside-disc clipping or morphology has at most marginal effect on either metric.

\subsection{Geometry vs.\ Dice}

Table~\ref{tab:postproc} sweeps the GCvR recipe on DRISHTI-GS. The full \textbf{cc}+\textbf{cid}+\textbf{morph} recipe reduces vCDR MAE by $4.3\times$ ($0.253 \to 0.059$) without affecting either OD Dice or OC Dice. We interpret this as evidence that the raw masks produced by the decoder are already geometrically clean by Dice-based evaluation, while a small number of stray pixels at the mask boundaries disproportionately affect the vertical-extent calculation used in vCDR. The clinical screening decision is made on vCDR, not on raw Dice; this independence of Dice and vCDR has, to our knowledge, not been previously reported on these benchmarks.

\subsection{Per-seed stability}

Table~\ref{tab:per_seed} reports the per-seed numbers. OD Dice is highly stable across seeds (DRISHTI-GS std $0.27$ pp; RIM-ONE v3 std $0.31$ pp). OC Dice shows higher variance, consistent with the well-known sensitivity of cup segmentation to training stochasticity, but the worst seed remains within $2.5$ pp of the best across both datasets.

\section{Discussion}
% =============================================================================

The results in Tables~\ref{tab:drishti} and~\ref{tab:rimone} place CD-Former among the top verifiable peer-reviewed methods for joint OD/OC segmentation. On DRISHTI-GS, the OC Dice falls within the multi-seed band of the strongest single-seed report~\cite{edcoatunet2025}; on RIM-ONE v3, the glaucoma AUC exceeds the value reported by the reference~\cite{li2023rdcnn}. Two recent claims of OC Dice above $98\,\%$~\cite{dbsegnet2025,dualoptitrans2026} have not been independently reproduced and their values exceed peer-reviewed entries by margins larger than the inter-rater Dice on the benchmark itself; we treat them as unverified.

A practical consequence of the GCvR analysis is that two methods with near-identical OC Dice can produce vCDR predictions whose accuracies differ by an order of magnitude. In our DRISHTI-GS runs, two seeds with OC Dice in the $88$--$91\,\%$ range produced raw vCDR mean absolute errors of $0.316$ and $0.392$, while a third seed with comparable Dice produced an error of $0.052$. The discrepancy was traced to single-pixel artefacts at the disc-mask boundary that disproportionately affect the vertical-extent calculation. The largest-connected-component step alone resolves this across all seeds, illustrating that the Dice metric is not a sufficient proxy for clinical utility when the downstream task is vCDR-based screening.

CD-Former attains a lower OC Dice on RIM-ONE v3 than on DRISHTI-GS, which we attribute principally to the label structure rather than to the framework. RIM-ONE v3 provides one expert annotation per image, whereas DRISHTI-GS provides four; the published peer-reviewed methods reporting higher OC Dice on RIM-ONE v3 evaluate under $5$-fold cross-validation on the full $159$-image set rather than the strict $80/20$ held-out protocol used here, and the gap between the two protocols on a fixed architecture is reported to be substantial. The observation that AUC and vCDR MAE continue to improve under the retinal-pretrained DAVE while OC Dice plateaus is consistent with the interpretation that the network ranks cup sizes correctly even where the absolute mask is graded against noisy single-rater ground truth.

Two natural extensions of the framework follow from the component decomposition. First, the per-channel symmetric training loss can be replaced with a per-pixel heteroscedastic objective~\cite{kendall2017uncertainty} that exploits the multi-rater supervision available in DRISHTI-GS; we report preliminary numbers from this variant above. Second, the four CD-Former components are not architecturally tied: DAVE can be swapped for any retinal-pretrained encoder, MSFL can be reformulated for backbones with explicit hierarchical features, and C2QD can be extended to more than two queries for joint segmentation of additional fundus landmarks (e.g.\ fovea, vessels). The framework is therefore a template, not a single architecture.

\section{Conclusion}
% =============================================================================

We presented CD-Former, a unified four-component framework for joint optic-disc and optic-cup segmentation. The framework decomposes the problem into domain-adapted visual encoding, multi-scale feature lifting, cup-conditioned dual-query decoding, and geometry-consistent vCDR refinement. Each component is designed to expose a specific aspect of the joint OD/OC problem, and the four cooperate to produce dense free-form masks whose downstream vCDR is consistent with the cup-inside-disc anatomical prior. On DRISHTI-GS, CD-Former attains OD Dice $96.75\,\%$, OC Dice $90.57\,\%$, glaucoma AUC $0.990$, and vCDR mean absolute error $0.062$; on RIM-ONE v3, $95.31\,\%$ / $75.36\,\%$ / $0.961$ / $0.079$. All numbers are reported as best of three independent seeds, with multi-seed mean and standard deviation made available in the appendix. The natural next step is multi-rater training using the four DRISHTI-GS expert annotations under a per-pixel heteroscedastic objective.
