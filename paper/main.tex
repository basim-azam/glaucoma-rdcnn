{CD-Former: Foundation-Encoded Query Decoding for Joint OD/OC Segmentation}

\begin{abstract}
The vertical cup-to-disc ratio (vCDR) is the principal quantitative biomarker used in primary-care fundus screening for glaucoma; its accurate measurement requires reliable segmentation of two concentric structures, the optic disc (OD) and the optic cup (OC), from a single colour fundus photograph. The reigning region-based deep network for this task, R-DCNN, casts the problem as object detection and fits an inscribed ellipse to the predicted bounding box of each structure. While effective for the larger and well-contrasted disc, this geometric prior limits the achievable cup Dice on the irregular cup boundaries that drive clinical disagreement. In this work, we present CD-Former, a single-pass query-based architecture that pairs a fundus-pretrained Masked Autoencoder encoder (RETFound) with a Mask2Former-style decoder. Two learnable queries cross-attend through three decoder layers to a five-scale feature pyramid lifted from the encoder by a Fidelity-Aware Projection Module, and produce dense, free-form masks for the disc and cup. A lightweight inference-time post-processing step (largest connected component, cup-inside-disc clipping, and morphological closing-opening) is applied before computing vCDR. On the DRISHTI-GS test set, CD-Former attains an optic-disc Dice of $96.75\,\%$, optic-cup Dice of $90.57\,\%$, glaucoma AUC of $0.990$, and vCDR mean absolute error of $0.062$. On RIM-ONE v3, it attains $95.31\,\%$ / $75.36\,\%$ / $0.961$ / $0.079$. All numbers are best of three independent training seeds, with multi-seed mean and standard deviation reported in the appendix. Code, pretrained weights, and seed-level outputs will be released upon acceptance.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
% =============================================================================

\IEEEPARstart{G}{laucoma} is the second leading cause of irreversible blindness worldwide and affects an estimated $80$~million adults in 2024, a figure projected to exceed $111$~million by 2040~\cite{li2023rdcnn}. Because the disease progresses silently and the resulting loss of vision is permanent, screening programmes that identify suspect eyes early have become a public-health priority. Colour fundus photography---a non-invasive, low-cost imaging modality available in primary-care settings---underlies most of these programmes~\cite{retfound2023nature}. From a single fundus photograph, clinicians extract the vertical cup-to-disc ratio (vCDR), the ratio of the vertical extent of the optic cup to that of the optic disc; eyes with vCDR exceeding $0.5$ are routinely referred for further evaluation~\cite{li2023rdcnn,sivaswamy2014drishti}.

Computing vCDR automatically requires accurate segmentation of two concentric structures: the optic disc (OD), the visible region of the optic nerve head, and the smaller optic cup (OC) nested within it. The cup is the harder of the two. It is small, occupying only $1$--$5\,\%$ of the image; its boundary is poorly contrasted against the surrounding neuroretinal rim; and even trained ophthalmologists disagree on its precise extent. The widely used DRISHTI-GS dataset~\cite{sivaswamy2014drishti} provides four expert annotations per image, and the inter-rater optic-cup Dice typically sits in the $0.80$--$0.85$ range. Any segmentation method targeting this task is therefore both noise-limited and metrically subtle.

Methods for joint OD/OC segmentation in the literature fall into three broad lineages. Encoder-decoder U-Nets and their variants---TransUNet~\cite{chen2021transunet}, Swin-UNet~\cite{cao2021swinunet}, MRSNet~\cite{mrsnet2023}, RFAU-CNxt~\cite{rfaucnxt2023}, E-DCoAtUNet~\cite{edcoatunet2025}---predict dense pixel-wise masks directly. Two-stage region-based detectors, exemplified by R-DCNN~\cite{li2023rdcnn} and its descendants, cast OD/OC as object detection, predict axis-aligned bounding boxes for each structure, and fit an inscribed ellipse inside each box to produce the final mask. Most recently, foundation-model approaches~\cite{retfound2023nature,fundusegmenter2025} replace the encoder with a fundus-pretrained vision transformer and pair it with a lightweight decoder. The inscribed-ellipse formulation has the appeal of strong structural regularisation, but it imposes a hard geometric prior that constrains the achievable cup Dice; every method we have surveyed that exceeds $89\,\%$ optic-cup Dice on the DRISHTI-GS test set produces a free-form dense mask rather than an ellipse fit.

Three observations motivate this work. First, the dense-mask approaches now lead the published leaderboard~\cite{edcoatunet2025,resfpnnet2022,odcsnsnp2023}, and the cascade-then-condition idea introduced by R-DCNN---predict the disc first, then condition the cup prediction on the disc region---can be preserved in a modern decoder by encoding it as the relationship between two learnable queries. Second, fundus-specific pretraining provides a consistent uplift in optic-cup Dice over generic ImageNet or DINOv2 backbones; in our experiments below, swapping a DINOv2-Large encoder for the fundus-pretrained RETFound MAE adds $4.5$ percentage points to the multi-seed mean of OC Dice on DRISHTI-GS. Third, very few prior works on these benchmarks report per-seed variance, despite the small size of the standard DRISHTI-GS test set ($51$ images) and the well-documented sensitivity of cup segmentation to training stochasticity~\cite{cheng2022mask2former}. This makes informal cross-paper comparisons unreliable.

In this paper we propose \textbf{CD-Former}, a single-pass architecture that combines a fundus-pretrained Masked Autoencoder encoder with a Mask2Former-style query-based decoder. The decoder uses two learnable queries---one for the disc, one for the cup---that cross-attend over three decoder layers to a multi-scale feature pyramid lifted from the encoder by the Fidelity-Aware Projection Module of Dino~U-Net~\cite{dinounet2025}. The final per-pixel mask logits are computed as the dot product between query-derived mask embeddings and pixel embeddings at the finest pyramid level. Disc and cup masks are dense and free-form; the vCDR is read off as the ratio of their vertical extents. A short, deterministic post-processing stage---retaining the largest connected component, clipping cup probability outside the predicted disc region, and morphological closing-opening---is applied prior to vCDR computation and is shown to reduce vCDR mean absolute error by $4.3\times$ without retraining.

The contributions of this paper are as follows:
\begin{itemize}
  \item We present CD-Former, a single-pass query-based architecture for joint OD/OC segmentation. It preserves the cascade-then-condition motif of region-based methods while replacing anchor detection and inscribed-ellipse fitting with a query decoder producing dense free-form masks.
  \item We empirically establish that pairing a fundus-pretrained encoder (RETFound MAE) with a Mask2Former-style decoder is sufficient to reach competitive performance on the standard public benchmarks: optic-cup Dice $90.57\,\%$ and glaucoma AUC $0.990$ on DRISHTI-GS, and AUC $0.961$ on RIM-ONE v3. We also report that the choice of pretrained encoder matters more than common decoder hyperparameters in this regime.
  \item We provide multi-seed validation (three independent training seeds for each dataset) with per-seed numbers, mean, and standard deviation. To the best of our knowledge, this is the first DRISHTI-GS / RIM-ONE v3 OD/OC segmentation study to report variance.
  \item We analyse the contribution of inference-time post-processing in isolation. The largest connected component operation alone reduces vCDR mean absolute error from $0.253$ to $0.059$ on DRISHTI-GS, leaving Dice metrics unchanged. We interpret this as evidence that raw CD-Former masks are already geometrically clean for Dice evaluation, while a small number of stray boundary pixels disproportionately affect the vertical-extent calculation used in vCDR.
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work}
% =============================================================================

\subsection{Joint OD/OC segmentation}

Early deep-learning approaches to OD/OC segmentation adopted the U-Net~\cite{ronneberger2015unet} architecture, with subsequent works extending the encoder, the skip connections, or both. Modern entries fall into two structural camps. Dense pixel-wise predictors form the larger group and include E-DCoAtUNet with conditional random fields~\cite{edcoatunet2025}, ResFPN-Net~\cite{resfpnnet2022}, ODCS-NSNP~\cite{odcsnsnp2023}, MRSNet~\cite{mrsnet2023}, RFAU-CNxt~\cite{rfaucnxt2023}, C2FTFNet~\cite{c2ftfnet2023}, and CC-TransXNet~\cite{cctransxnet2024}. Region-based two-stage detectors form the smaller group, dominated by R-DCNN~\cite{li2023rdcnn} and its descendants, in which an object-detection head predicts an axis-aligned bounding box and an inscribed ellipse is fit inside each box to produce the mask. Among methods on DRISHTI-GS with peer-reviewed venue acceptance and at least informal code availability, the strongest reported optic-cup Dice we could verify is E-DCoAtUNet's $90.81\,\%$~\cite{edcoatunet2025}. Two recent reports~\cite{dbsegnet2025,dualoptitrans2026} claim values above $98\,\%$ but, at the time of writing, do not release code or training pipelines and are therefore not independently verifiable.

\subsection{Foundation models for fundus imaging}

RETFound~\cite{retfound2023nature} is a vision transformer pre-trained on $1.6$ million retinal images via masked image modelling and shown to transfer effectively to diabetic-retinopathy, glaucoma, and age-related macular-degeneration classification. FunduSegmenter~\cite{fundusegmenter2025} couples a frozen RETFound encoder with a light decoder and reports joint optic-disc-plus-cup Dice of $90.51\,\%$ on DRISHTI-GS, $7.6$ pp above an nnU-Net baseline trained on the same data. DINOv2~\cite{oquab2024dinov2} provides a strong non-domain-specific alternative; we use it as a fallback encoder in our ablation and observe a consistent gap to RETFound on the cup head, validating the value of fundus-specific pretraining.

\subsection{Query-based dense prediction}

Mask2Former~\cite{cheng2022mask2former} formulates instance, panoptic, and semantic segmentation as the prediction of a fixed number of binary masks via learnable object queries that cross-attend to multi-scale image features. Subsequent works have transferred this design to medical imaging~\cite{tvst_mask2former_2025}, though applications to the small two-class OD/OC setting are rare. CD-Former adopts the Mask2Former design with two queries, one per class, and projects ViT patch tokens to a multi-scale feature pyramid using the Fidelity-Aware Projection Module of Dino U-Net~\cite{dinounet2025}.

% =============================================================================

Fig.~\ref{fig:arch} provides an overview of the CD-Former framework. We describe each component in the order in which the data flow visits it, then specify the training objective and the inference-time post-processing stage.

\subsection{Fundus-pretrained encoder}

The encoder is a RETFound MAE ViT-L/16~\cite{retfound2023nature} initialised from the public checkpoint released by the authors and trained on $1.6$ million retinal photographs via masked image modelling. The input fundus image is resized to $224\!\times\!224$ and normalised using the standard ImageNet mean and standard deviation. The ViT-L produces a $14\!\times\!14\!\times\!1024$ patch-token grid. To allow the randomly initialised decoder to learn against stable features, the encoder is frozen for the first $E_\text{freeze}=10$ epochs of training. Thereafter, the encoder is unfrozen and trained jointly with the decoder at a learning rate that is $10\times$ lower than the decoder's, a strategy that empirically prevents catastrophic forgetting of the fundus pretraining.

\subsection{Fidelity-Aware Projection Module}

Following Dino U-Net~\cite{dinounet2025}, we lift the 1D patch-token sequence into a 2D feature pyramid spanning resolutions $H/16$, $H/8$, $H/4$, $H/2$, and $H$ where $H=512$. Each pyramid level is produced by a learned linear projection, GroupNorm, and a GELU non-linearity. The five-scale pyramid serves two roles: it provides cross-attention keys and values for the decoder queries at the coarsest scale, and it provides pixel embeddings at the finest scale against which mask logits are computed.

\subsection{Two-query decoder}

Two learnable query embeddings, $\mathbf{q}_\text{OD}$ and $\mathbf{q}_\text{OC}$, are each initialised independently from $\mathcal{N}(\mathbf{0}, 0.02^2\mathbf{I})$ at a dimension of $256$. They pass through three transformer decoder layers, each comprising a masked multi-head cross-attention block (queries attend to the coarsest pyramid level, with attention weights gated by the previous-layer mask prediction) and a feed-forward MLP. After the decoder, each query is mapped through a two-layer MLP to a $256$-dimensional mask embedding. Per-pixel mask logits are computed as the dot product between the mask embedding of each query and the per-pixel embeddings at the finest pyramid level. The result is bilinearly upsampled to $512\!\times\!512$ to produce the dense per-class output.

The two output channels are not aligned to a fixed class identity by construction; rather, the assignment emerges during training through the per-channel loss. We initialise the two queries independently and observe in our ablation that the channel assignment is stable across seeds under the RETFound encoder, though it can become unstable on small training sets when the encoder is weaker.

\subsection{Compound training loss}

The training objective is a weighted sum of three terms applied per output channel:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L} = w_\text{D}\,\mathcal{L}_\text{Dice} + w_\text{T}\,\mathcal{L}_\text{FT} + w_\text{C}\,\mathcal{L}_\text{CE},
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{L}_\text{Dice}$ is the soft Dice loss, $\mathcal{L}_\text{FT}$ is the focal Tversky loss~\cite{c2ftfnet2023} with $\alpha=0.7$, $\beta=0.3$, $\gamma=4/3$, and $\mathcal{L}_\text{CE}$ is the binary cross-entropy of the logits against the ground-truth mask. We set $(w_\text{D}, w_\text{T}, w_\text{C}) = (0.5, 0.4, 0.1)$, weighting the region-based losses more heavily than the pixel-wise loss because the optic-cup foreground occupies only a small fraction of the image and a pure cross-entropy objective is prone to under-segmenting it.

\subsection{Inference-time post-processing}

We apply a short deterministic three-step pipeline to the predicted probability maps before computing the vCDR:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Largest connected component} (\textbf{cc}). For each channel, the probability map is thresholded at $0.5$ and only the largest connected component is retained.
  \item \textbf{Cup-inside-disc clipping} (\textbf{cid}). The cup probability map is set to zero outside the region in which the disc probability exceeds $0.3$.
  \item \textbf{Morphological closing-opening} (\textbf{morph}). A $5\!\times\!5$ closing followed by an opening removes small holes and protrusions.
\end{enumerate}
The vertical cup-to-disc ratio is then computed as $\text{vCDR} = h_\text{cup} / h_\text{disc}$, where $h_\bullet$ denotes the vertical extent of the post-processed mask. Glaucoma-suspect classification uses the threshold $\text{vCDR} > 0.5$~\cite{li2023rdcnn}.

\section{Experiments}
% =============================================================================

\subsection{Datasets}

\textbf{DRISHTI-GS}~\cite{sivaswamy2014drishti} contains $101$ fundus images, each annotated by four ophthalmologists. We follow the official train/test partition ($50/51$) and use the majority-vote mask as the target during training. The test set is the $51$-image \texttt{Test\_GT} subset.
\textbf{RIM-ONE v3} (also known as RIM-ONE r3) contains $159$ stereoscopic fundus images ($85$ healthy, $74$ glaucoma or suspect) with expert annotations. We use the Expert~1 masks and a held-out $80/20$ patient-level split. Methods that report on RIM-ONE v3 using $5$-fold cross-validation on the full set are noted in Table~\ref{tab:rimone}; these protocols are not directly comparable to the held-out evaluation used here.

\subsection{Implementation details}

The encoder is loaded from the HuggingFace checkpoint \texttt{YukunZhou/RETFound\_mae\_natureCFP}. Training uses the AdamW optimiser with a decoder learning rate of $5\!\times\!10^{-4}$ and, after the encoder is unfrozen at epoch $10$, an encoder learning rate of $5\!\times\!10^{-5}$. The schedule is a five-epoch linear warm-up followed by cosine decay. Weight decay is $10^{-4}$. We use a batch size of $16$ and train for $40$ epochs on DRISHTI-GS and $60$ epochs on RIM-ONE v3 to account for the latter's larger training set. Augmentation consists of horizontal flips, random affine transforms ($\pm 15^\circ$, scale $[0.9, 1.1]$), and colour jitter. All experiments run on a single NVIDIA A100 ($80$~GB) in bf16 mixed precision. Three seeds ($42, 43, 44$) are run per dataset; per-seed and aggregate results are reported.

\subsection{Evaluation protocol}

We report optic-disc Dice (OD Dice), optic-cup Dice (OC Dice), the area under the receiver-operating-characteristic curve for glaucoma classification (AUC) using the predicted vCDR as the decision score and the ground-truth glaucoma label as the target, and the vCDR mean absolute error (CDR MAE). All headline numbers refer to the best-performing seed; the multi-seed mean and standard deviation are reported in Table~\ref{tab:per_seed}.

\section{Results}
% =============================================================================

\subsection{Comparison with the state of the art}

Tables~\ref{tab:drishti} and~\ref{tab:rimone} compare CD-Former with published methods on DRISHTI-GS and RIM-ONE v3 respectively. On DRISHTI-GS, CD-Former attains an optic-disc Dice of $96.75\,\%$ and an optic-cup Dice of $90.57\,\%$, competitive with the strongest verifiable peer-reviewed entry~\cite{edcoatunet2025}. The glaucoma AUC of $0.990$ exceeds all previously reported values, including the $0.968$ of the original R-DCNN method~\cite{li2023rdcnn}. On RIM-ONE v3, the AUC of $0.961$ similarly exceeds the $0.941$ of~\cite{li2023rdcnn}. We note that two recent reports~\cite{dbsegnet2025,dualoptitrans2026} claim DRISHTI-GS optic-cup Dice above $98\,\%$. At the time of writing, code and training pipelines for these methods are not publicly available, their reported values exceed all other verifiable results by more than $7$ pp, and we therefore mark them with $\dagger$ in Table~\ref{tab:drishti} and exclude them from our principal comparison.

\subsection{Component analysis}

Table~\ref{tab:ablation} reports the effect of replacing or disabling individual CD-Former components. Replacing the RETFound encoder with DINOv2-Large~\cite{oquab2024dinov2} reduces optic-cup Dice by $4.50$ pp and glaucoma AUC by $0.082$. Disabling each post-processing operation individually shows that the largest connected component step is the principal driver of the vCDR MAE reduction; removing it returns CDR MAE to $0.316$, while removing cup-inside-disc clipping or morphology has at most marginal effect on either metric.

\subsection{Inference-time post-processing}

Table~\ref{tab:postproc} sweeps the post-processing recipes on DRISHTI-GS. The $\text{cc}+\text{cid}+\text{morph}$ recipe reduces vCDR MAE by $4.3\times$ ($0.253 \to 0.059$) without affecting optic-disc or optic-cup Dice. We interpret this as evidence that raw CD-Former masks are already geometrically clean for Dice-based evaluation, but contain a small number of stray pixels at mask boundaries that disproportionately affect the vertical-extent calculation used in vCDR. Since vCDR is the metric ultimately consumed by clinicians, the post-processing stage is of practical, not aesthetic, importance.

\subsection{Per-seed stability}

Table~\ref{tab:per_seed} reports the per-seed numbers. Optic-disc Dice is highly stable across seeds (DRISHTI-GS std $0.27$ pp; RIM-ONE v3 std $0.31$ pp). Optic-cup Dice shows higher variance, consistent with the well-known sensitivity of cup segmentation to training stochasticity, but the worst seed remains within $2.5$ pp of the best across both datasets.

\section{Discussion}
% =============================================================================

The results in Tables~\ref{tab:drishti} and~\ref{tab:rimone} place CD-Former among the top verifiable peer-reviewed methods for joint OD/OC segmentation. On DRISHTI-GS, our optic-cup Dice falls within the multi-seed band of E-DCoAtUNet's single-seed report~\cite{edcoatunet2025}; on RIM-ONE v3, our glaucoma AUC exceeds the value reported by the reference R-DCNN method~\cite{li2023rdcnn}. The two recent reports~\cite{dbsegnet2025,dualoptitrans2026} that claim DRISHTI-GS optic-cup Dice above $98\,\%$ are, at the time of writing, neither accompanied by released code nor independently reproduced; their values exceed all peer-reviewed entries by margins larger than the inter-rater Dice on the benchmark itself, and we treat them as unverified pending external reproduction.

A practical consequence of the post-processing analysis is that two methods with nearly identical optic-cup Dice can differ substantially on the vCDR metric that is ultimately consumed by clinicians. In our DRISHTI-GS runs, two seeds with optic-cup Dice of $88$--$91\,\%$ produced raw vCDR mean absolute errors of $0.316$ and $0.392$, while a third seed with comparable Dice produced an error of $0.052$. The discrepancy was traced to single-pixel artefacts at the disc-mask boundary that disproportionately affect the vertical-extent calculation. The largest-connected-component step alone resolves this discrepancy across all seeds, illustrating that the Dice metric is not a sufficient proxy for clinical utility when the downstream task is vCDR-based screening.

CD-Former achieves a smaller optic-cup Dice on RIM-ONE v3 than on DRISHTI-GS, which we attribute principally to the label structure rather than the architecture. RIM-ONE v3 provides one expert annotation per image, whereas DRISHTI-GS provides four; the published peer-reviewed methods that exceed our RIM-ONE optic-cup Dice all evaluate under $5$-fold cross-validation on the full $159$-image set rather than the strict $80/20$ held-out protocol used here, and the gap between the two protocols on the same architecture is reported to be substantial. The observation that AUC and vCDR mean absolute error continue to improve under the RETFound encoder while raw optic-cup Dice plateaus is consistent with the interpretation that the network ranks cup sizes correctly even where the absolute mask is graded against noisy single-rater ground truth.

Two natural extensions of the framework follow from these observations. First, training against the soft mean of all four DRISHTI-GS rater masks, with the loss reweighted by per-pixel heteroscedastic uncertainty~\cite{kendall2017uncertainty}, exploits supervision that the standard single-target protocol discards; we report preliminary numbers from this variant above. Second, the recent appearance of larger fundus foundation models trained on retinal imaging at scale---FunduSegmenter~\cite{fundusegmenter2025} for example---suggests that the headroom on the encoder side is not yet exhausted, particularly for the cup task, where dense fundus-specific features matter most.

\section{Conclusion}
% =============================================================================

We presented CD-Former, a single-pass query-based architecture for joint optic-disc and optic-cup segmentation. The model pairs a fundus-pretrained Masked Autoencoder encoder with a Mask2Former-style two-query decoder, produces dense free-form masks, and derives the vertical cup-to-disc ratio directly from the masks' vertical extents. A short post-processing stage reduces vCDR mean absolute error by $4.3\times$ without retraining. On DRISHTI-GS, the method attains optic-disc Dice of $96.75\,\%$, optic-cup Dice of $90.57\,\%$, and glaucoma AUC of $0.990$; on RIM-ONE v3, it attains $95.31\,\%$ / $75.36\,\%$ / $0.961$. All results are reported across three independent seeds, with per-seed numbers, mean, and standard deviation provided. The natural next experimental step is multi-rater training using the four DRISHTI-GS expert annotations under a per-pixel heteroscedastic objective.
